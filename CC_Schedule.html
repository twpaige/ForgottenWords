Generation first · 26 weeks

Six-Month Development Roadmap

Prove the world. Complete its generation. Then build the game.

The priority is the world itself

Weeks 1–10 belong exclusively to the complete generative system: physical world facts, graphical maps, perception-filtered LOOK prose, environmental inputs and the infrastructure needed to run and verify them. The first working map-and-LOOK demonstration is due in week 2.

Hard dependency: no gameplay work before Gate A.

Accounts, player movement, inventory, crafting, combat, NPC behavior, economy and progression start only after the complete generative foundation is running and accepted. The early viewer may move an inspection cursor and use synthetic entities/changes to test world output; those are generation test tools, not early gameplay systems.

This is a proposed 26-week plan, approximately six months from a start date to be chosen. It assumes sustained development effort, timely design decisions and weekly review by Thomas. It is a sequencing and acceptance plan, not a staffing estimate or a guarantee that the complete design fits six months. Weeks 19–22 are particularly ambitious.

Gate A · What “generation complete” means

The target is the complete generative foundation required by the current design, not every coordinate precomputed and not merely an attractive screenshot. On-demand generation is the intended architecture. Gameplay events later supply new facts to the same completed map/perception/prose pipeline.

  • One authoritative world: seed and coordinates reproduce physical facts. Map and LOOK consume those facts; LOOK does not create geography.
  • Full geographic coverage: required landforms, hydrology, vegetation, habitat/resource placement, roads, structures and starting-world features are implemented. Their boundaries and relationships are coherent.
  • Complete presentation: map scales/layers and full/BRIEF prose cover supported scenes, interiors, distant landmarks and environmental conditions. Descriptions communicate relationships rather than disconnected fields.
  • Perception correctness: SENSE, LOS, distance, concealment, light and weather determine what can be described. Fixture entities exercise the full presentation contract without requiring live NPCs.
  • Factual consistency: a repeatable corpus covers coast, water, forest, open terrain, slopes, roads, settlement/interior, day/night, weather and chunk-edge scenes. Map, LOOK and BRIEF disagree on no underlying facts or entitled information.
  • Persistence and reproducibility: restart, cache eviction and regeneration retain synthetic world changes; generation-version behavior is documented and tested before persistent play begins.
  • Measured operation: cold/warm query timings, memory use, distant-landmark queries and the chosen load target pass budgets agreed on the target machine. Exact numbers are measured and recorded, not invented by this roadmap.
  • A usable running deliverable: Thomas can independently launch or access the explorer, inspect any supported test location, compare map and prose, and reproduce a reported defect by seed/coordinate/conditions.
Acceptance evidence: running build + scene corpus + consistency checks + performance report + restart/overlay results + Thomas’s review. An incomplete generation item keeps Gate A closed.

Weeks 1–10 · Highest priority

Stage 1 · Prove and complete generation

Build the entire generative foundation first. A standalone exploration viewer is the running product for this stage. Its inspection cursor is a development tool, not a player character. No account, inventory, crafting, combat, economy or progression implementation begins before Gate A passes.

Week 1 · World facts and inspection harness

Build & verify
Define the seed/coordinate contract, inch-based signed 64-bit X/Y/Z model, world-fact schema and reproducible fixtures. Build only the runner and map/LOOK inspection shell needed to exercise generation.

Weekly acceptance
One seed and coordinate produce inspectable physical facts. Map and prose consume the same facts; neither invents terrain independently.

Week 2 · First map + LOOK proof

Build & verify
Generate a small traversable-looking sample containing contrasting terrain and a connected landmark relationship. Display its map and substantial LOOK prose together; support selecting locations and BRIEF.

Weekly acceptance
Thomas can inspect several positions and see that directions, slope, vegetation and landmarks agree. Repeated LOOKs retain the same facts. This is an early proof, not permission to start gameplay.

Week 3 · Landform generation

Build & verify
Complete base elevation, slopes, coastlines and geographic continuity across the supported world. Exercise adjacent chunks and large coordinates.

Weekly acceptance
Seed fixtures reproduce the same landforms across chunk boundaries, request order and process restarts.

Week 4 · Water and ecological geography

Build & verify
Complete hydrology and biome/vegetation/resource placement required by the design. Derive relationships from geography rather than independent random labels.

Weekly acceptance
Water, terrain, vegetation and habitat facts form coherent scenes and agree between map and LOOK. Edge cases are saved as repeatable fixtures.

Week 5 · Built geography and world features

Build & verify
Complete required initial roads, Origin/settlement placement, structures, interiors and persistent feature identities. Hand-authored starting content may coexist with procedural geography.

Weekly acceptance
Roads, buildings, entrances and interiors occupy consistent real geometry. Initial world content is usable by both presentation systems; no commerce or NPC behavior is implemented yet.

Week 6 · Perception and environmental inputs

Build & verify
Finish generation-facing LOS, occlusion, horizon detail, light and weather inputs, SENSE filtering and distance-based detail. Represent entities with fixtures for description testing.

Weekly acceptance
Walls, darkness, distance and concealment suppress unavailable information. Seeing a light differs from seeing by it. The player-facing view does not expose the diagnostic map's privileged facts.

Week 7 · Complete prose composition

Build & verify
Finish relational scene composition, ordering, transitions, variation and BRIEF. Cover terrain, water, roads, buildings, distant landmarks, environment and fixture entities.

Weekly acceptance
Descriptions read as connected places, not field dumps. BRIEF and full LOOK convey the same entitled information; wording variation cannot change physical facts.

Week 8 · Complete map presentation

Build & verify
Finish graphical map rendering, scale/zoom, bearings, selected-point inspection and all required generated feature layers. Keep diagnostic visibility separate from eventual in-game map access.

Weekly acceptance
Map and LOOK agree across the scene corpus. Navigation is legible at supported scales. This completes the map engine; physical map items, purchase and crafting remain later gameplay.

Week 9 · Generation persistence and performance

Build & verify
Benchmark chunk size/cache policy after geography is substantially complete. Finish cache eviction/reconstruction, persistent-change overlays, generator version handling and restart behavior using synthetic changes.

Weekly acceptance
Cold and warm queries, boundary crossings and restarts pass measured budgets agreed for the target machine. Evicting a cache cannot erase a removed tree or added structure fixture.

Week 10 · Generation acceptance and running release

Build & verify
Run the complete scene/seed regression corpus, map/prose comparisons, soak and restart tests; correct defects and package the standalone explorer with repeatable run instructions.

Weekly acceptance
GATE A: Thomas reviews the running complete generative system. Every Gate A acceptance item passes. Gameplay work is blocked until this gate is accepted.

Weeks 11–14 · Only after Gate A

Stage 2 · Establish the persistent player loop

Connect the proven world to real characters. Reuse the generation and perception services; do not replace them with a second room-based world.

Week 11 · Sessions, identity and character creation

Build & verify
Implement account policy, character creation/CAP allocation, command dispatch, browser text interface, save/load, reconnect and administrative access.

Weekly acceptance
A character can enter the accepted generated world, LOOK, disconnect and return with identity and state intact.

Week 12 · Travel and player perception

Build & verify
Implement continuous movement, posture, terrain/water boundaries, stamina and reference-weight encumbrance. Connect LOOK/SCAN/SEARCH/WATCH/TRACK, trails, private recognition labels and communication.

Weekly acceptance
Travel, observation and messages respect geometry and perception. Confirm tuning inputs before applying them; test darkness, obstructions, labels and trail loss.

Week 13 · Physical possessions and equipment

Build & verify
Implement hands, grip, wielding, layered wear, recursive containers, targeting, weight/capacity, drop/get/put and visible states.

Weekly acceptance
Possessions remain physically consistent through nested moves, full hands, worn layers and restart. No duplicate, lost or circularly contained items.

Week 14 · Crafts, learning and navigation loop

Build & verify
Implement deterministic work/learning timers, material access, initial gathering, fire/food/water foundations, physical maps, bookmarks, compasses and tents; use a controlled supplier fixture until commerce is built.

Weekly acceptance
A character travels two miles, pitches and marks a tent, loses sight of it, navigates back and finds it after restart. Hunting completion waits for combat; no temporary competing combat model.

Weeks 15–18 · Rules fidelity before expansion

Stage 3 · Combat and the complete survival slice

Implement Crown & Call's current turn-based rules directly, then the CCMUD encounter policies. The obsolete declaration/server-batch model is excluded.

Week 15 · Turn-based rules engine

Build & verify
Implement initiative, MOVE/PREP/ACT, HOLD, attacks, defenses, ranges, weapons/armor and ENGAGED/WITHDRAW from CC_Ruleset. Add rules-based examples and regression tests.

Weekly acceptance
Deterministic test encounters match the authoritative rules, including movement restrictions and legal action timing.

Week 16 · Multiplayer encounter orchestration

Build & verify
Implement combat zones, legal late entry, merged encounters, link-dead behavior, timeouts and anonymous STOP/DENY including NPC votes.

Weekly acceptance
Multi-character scenarios merge and end correctly without encounter splitting, leaked vote identities or extra reconnect time. Resolve documented scheduling/geometry choices before encoding them.

Week 17 · Injury, prisoners and death

Build & verify
Implement wounds, Mortal/unconscious/bleed-out, REVIVE, SUBDUE, adjacent SURRENDER, automatic binding, noncombatant prisoners, forced-follow and release. Connect established PC death aftermath.

Weekly acceptance
Test interruption and restart during dying, surrender and follow. Death drops possessions without inventing PC corpses; established death/return policies remain authoritative.

Week 18 · Wildlife and full starter survival

Build & verify
Implement FLUSH habitat eligibility, Work Timer and temporary-animal lifecycle; complete hunting, rabbit processing, primitive tools, fire, cooking and waterskin paths with finalized content tables.

Weekly acceptance
GATE B: Normal and Naked starter paths work end to end, including combat and survival. The rabbit and tent/navigation loops both survive reconnect and restart.

Weeks 19–22 · Feature completion target

Stage 4 · Complete the designed social world

Add the remaining defined systems against the same persistence and rules foundations. This is the most compressed stage; carry incomplete work forward visibly rather than declaring it finished.

Week 19 · Origin services, economy and advancement

Build & verify
Implement required merchants, prices/stock policy, money, banking, NPC services, CAP Boost Tokens, prerequisites and remaining defined Craft/Calling effects. Audit coverage against both source documents.

Weekly acceptance
Origin supports the starter loop without supplier fixtures. Trades and advancement are atomic and auditable; idle time and command spam do not substitute for meaningful play.

Week 20 · Property, construction and succession

Build & verify
Implement remaining defined construction, keyless door permissions/bans, deeds/claims, ordered heirs, eviction, front-door sale, virtual tours and approved property descriptions.

Weekly acceptance
Clock-controlled tests cover overlapping heir eligibility, ownership changes, eviction and forfeited contents. Preserve established bank inheritance and death aftermath.

Week 21 · Languages, writing and publication

Build & verify
Implement language capabilities, speech/text comprehension, signs, books/pages, ink, editing, copying, translation, publication and nested provenance.

Weekly acceptance
The numbered writing decisions pass scenario checks: moved pages and copied books retain lineage; reading restrictions and publication rules remain consistent.

Week 22 · Remaining content and administration

Build & verify
Complete the coverage audit, remaining explicitly in-scope content/abilities, moderation, art restrictions, staff review, help and operational controls. Address outstanding accepted features from earlier weeks.

Weekly acceptance
GATE C: Every in-scope design requirement has an implementation and verification reference. Outstanding or deferred decisions are named; no feature-complete claim while required work is missing.

Weeks 23–26 · Acceptance, not new feature expansion

Stage 5 · Prove reliability and release

Tests accompany every week. These final weeks provide integrated validation, player feedback and release reserve rather than postponing all quality work until the end.

Week 23 · Integration, persistence and recovery

Build & verify
Run cross-system journeys; test interrupted trades, crafting, combat, ownership transfer and document edits. Exercise backups, restore, upgrades and agreed crash-recovery behavior.

Weekly acceptance
A restore rehearsal succeeds. Critical state transitions preserve possessions, money, characters and world changes without duplication or silent loss.

Week 24 · Thomas-only production rehearsal

Build & verify
Deploy the release candidate for Thomas-only play under the design's access model. Measure real sessions, performance and usability; fix blockers and document operations.

Weekly acceptance
Thomas can complete the core loops without administrator intervention. Runbooks cover starting, stopping, backup, restore and diagnosis.

Week 25 · Invited private alpha

Build & verify
If the previous gate passes, admit a small invited group within the agreed initial capacity. Test concurrency, encounters, trade, communication, moderation and reconnects.

Weekly acceptance
Player feedback and measured behavior meet the agreed capacity and reliability targets. Access does not widen while blocking defects remain.

Week 26 · Release reserve and handover

Build & verify
Use the reserved week for fixes, retests, a final backup/restore check, documentation and requirements sign-off. Produce the six-month status and the next backlog.

Weekly acceptance
GATE D: Accept the stable private release only when required features and release checks pass. Otherwise keep the current safe access level and publish the remaining schedule.

Keep the plan honest

Priority and dependency order

Complete generation → persistent characters and physical interaction → authoritative combat and survival → remaining social/content systems → integrated acceptance and private release. Within each stage, correctness and recoverable persistent state take priority over polish. Optional audio and other explicitly deferred features do not displace required work.

Design decisions are dependencies

This schedule does not amend the rules. Use CCMUD_Design.txt C01–C11 and the preserved numbered decisions, with CC_Ruleset.html authoritative for combat. Keep explicitly obsolete rules excluded. Maintain a requirement-to-week-and-test ledger so a large feature cannot disappear under “remaining work.”

  • During generation: establish supported world coverage, environmental inputs, resource-change semantics needed by generation, performance budgets and generator-version/recovery contracts. Chunk/cache choices follow geographic benchmarks.
  • Before the affected gameplay week: confirm still-unset reference weight/stamina values, recipes/yields/times, compass/tent content, combat timing/zone/merge integration, merchant economy, return/claim edge cases and provenance visibility against existing sources.
  • Preserve deferrals: do not quietly implement speculative mechanics or label unresolved choices as settled. Log a blocking decision and bring it to Thomas when needed; this roadmap itself supplies no replacement rule.

Weekly review

Demonstrate the running increment, record passed/failed acceptance checks, update requirement coverage and name the next dependency. Write meaningful tests alongside each system. End-of-project testing supplements those checks rather than replacing them.

If a gate or week slips

Move dependent work forward in time. Do not start gameplay early to compensate for unfinished generation. Reforecast after weeks 2, 10, 18 and 22. Week 26 is an explicit one-week reserve, not enough to absorb major missing systems. If the full defined scope cannot pass by week 26, agree a revised completion date or an explicitly narrower milestone; never silently redefine “complete game” as “private alpha.”

Six-month outcome

The target is a feature-complete, verified private release of the currently defined scope, following the existing development → Thomas-only → invited-player rollout. Public launch is not automatically authorized by this date. Broader future systems and explicit design deferrals remain visible in the backlog.
